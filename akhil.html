Research in progress

Cash as a financing buffer in constrained Indian firms.

"Cash as a Financing Buffer: Precautionary Cash Holdings, Financing Uncertainty, and the Investment Protection Channel in Financially Constrained Indian Firms, 2010 to 2024"

Proposal, September 2026. Under the guidance of Prof. Lincy P. T., Research Faculty, XIME Kochi. Approximately four months to completion.

The persistent puzzle

Non-financial constituents of the S&P BSE 500 held cash and cash equivalents equal to approximately 11 percent of total assets in September 2024, materially consistent with the 12 percent documented by Arora (2019) for the same index in 2015. This persistence is anomalous. The corporate bond market in India remains at approximately 17 percent of GDP, well below China (37 percent), South Korea (79 percent), and the United States (46 percent). Bank credit is subject to statutory pre-commitments (SLR 18 percent, CRR 4 percent, priority sector at 40 percent of net bank credit). These conditions plausibly bind even solvent firms.

Questions

Does the elevated cash reflect the precautionary motive predicted by standard corporate finance theory, or agency-driven hoarding, or a pecking-order residual? If precautionary, does the buffer scale with the specific risks it is meant to hedge, namely firm-level cash flow volatility and macroeconomic policy uncertainty? Does the balance deliver observable investment protection during exogenous credit-supply contractions?

Method

Panel fixed-effects estimation on a Prowess-derived sample of approximately 2,000 to 2,500 non-financial listed firms over 2010 to 2024. Financial constraint status is measured through three independent proxies (Kaplan-Zingales, Whited-Wu, and Hadlock-Pierce size-age indices) for robustness. A difference-in-differences design around the September 2018 IL&FS default and the ensuing NBFC funding freeze provides exogenous shock-based identification for the investment protection channel. IL&FS held an AAA rating until weeks before its default, satisfying the exogeneity requirement.

Hypotheses

  • H1Financially constrained firms hold higher cash-to-assets ratios than unconstrained firms.
  • H2The differential widens with firm-level cash flow volatility.
  • H3The differential widens with macroeconomic policy uncertainty (BBD India EPU index).
  • H4Business-group-affiliated constrained firms hold lower balances, consistent with internal-capital-market substitution.
  • H5Constrained firms with above-median pre-shock cash sustain capital expenditure better through the NBFC funding freeze than constrained firms with below-median holdings.

Contribution

The Indian literature has separately established that constrained firms hold more cash on average (Arora 2019; Maheshwari and Rao 2017), that macroeconomic uncertainty raises cash demand, and that banking relationship structure affects cash levels. What has not been undertaken in a single integrated study is the joint examination of whether the buffer scales with the specific risks it hedges, whether it delivers observable investment protection under a credit-supply shock, and whether business-group affiliation substitutes for cash internally. The paper addresses that three-part gap.

Applied work

Selected financial models and engagements.

I. Laurus Labs equity valuation

Capstone project, PGDM Finance

Recommendation: HOLD. Blended target Rs. 1,368, a 13.8 percent downside against a CMP of Rs. 1,587. The valuation triangulated a DCF intrinsic value of Rs. 497 per share against relative and precedent transaction valuations in the Rs. 1,700 to Rs. 1,900 range. The DCF-versus-multiples gap was presented directly rather than smoothed into a single point estimate.

The 20-sheet DCF-FCFF model was built on a four-segment revenue architecture, applying the FMVA valuation framework to a live equity mandate. The core thesis, quantified in the model, was the transition of the business mix toward Contract Development and Manufacturing (CDMO) at approximately 50 percent of revenue by FY30, with the associated EBITDA margin expanding from approximately 27 percent to 33 percent by FY29.

Policy risk was modelled as a live scenario variable. Section 232 of the US Trade Expansion Act of 1962 grants the executive branch authority to impose tariffs on imports deemed a threat to national security. Its extension to pharmaceutical inputs was a live and evolving risk during the valuation window, with material implications for Indian generic exporters. The model stress-tested FY31 estimated revenue across Bull, Base, and Bear cases, combined with terminal WACC-g grids, producing an intrinsic value band of Rs. 332 to Rs. 709 per share.

II. RVR Projects: project finance underwriting

Financial Analyst Intern, RVR Projects Pvt. Ltd. (Hyderabad). Rs. 556 Cr HUDCO-funded water infrastructure EPC, Nellore. May to July 2026.

Built a project finance model on a live Rs. 556 Cr HUDCO-funded urban water EPC scheme in Nellore. The model covered Sources and Uses, an IDC-capitalised debt schedule, DSCR-sculpted debt sizing, and Project and Equity IRR sensitivities.

The build required calibration against the underlying credit and legal architecture: the interaction of HUDCO's sanction terms with the borrower's contracted revenue stream, the accounting treatment of interest during construction under Indian norms, the sculpting of DSCR against the tenor and amortisation of the debt tranches, and the sensitivity of Project IRR and Equity IRR to refinancing risk and delayed cash flows.

Credit surveillance included review of draft loan term sheets, financial covenant tracking, and modelling of macro-shock impact on debt-service capacity. A driver-based corporate financial model with three-statement projections and a DCF valuation was complemented by a Python-based Monte Carlo simulation across 10,000 trials, stress-testing performance under growth, margin, and cost-of-capital scenarios. Historical operating statements from FY22 to FY25 were reviewed and benchmarked against five listed peers on growth, profitability, leverage, and return.

The engagement concluded with a 30-slide financing recommendation to senior leadership, proposing a Rs. 1,200 Cr hybrid capital deployment. The recommendation was modelled to lift DCF equity value from Rs. 5,281 Cr to approximately Rs. 6,800 Cr by FY30, a 29 percent increase.
